# Lumen Onboarding Assistant — Verbatim Demo Script (Apple example, English)

How to read this:
- Normal paragraphs are your lines. Read them out loud, word for word.
- Lines that start with ▸ are stage directions. Do NOT read these aloud. They tell you what to click or what the screen shows.
- [SLIDE] means you are on a slide. [TOOL] means you are showing the live tool.

Apple is used as an illustrative example with public information only. Frame it as "an example client," not a real Apple account.

---

## BEFORE YOU PRESENT (about 15 minutes, on the laptop you will use on stage)

1. Make sure the latest version is deployed (Claude will confirm this once the fix is pushed).
2. Open the tool's Sales page. Enter the client and paste the two blocks from the bottom of this doc (the brief and the notes). Company is Apple, language English. Click Generate link.
3. Open that link and have the full conversation to the end, answering as if you are Apple. Leave that browser tab open. This finished conversation is what you reveal on stage.
4. Optional: open a second client tab and switch it to Arabic, so you have an Arabic screen ready to flash.
5. Record a screen capture of one full run as your backup.
6. Pre-open these tabs, in this order: Sales page, a fresh Apple client link, the finished conversation from step 3, the Dashboard.
7. Zoom the browser to about 125 percent. Turn off notifications.

## THE SCRIPT

### Problem  [SLIDE]  (about 2 minutes)

Hi everyone. I want to show you how Professional Services is using AI to speed up onboarding and get clients to value faster. The tool is the Lumen Onboarding Assistant. We recently tested it with a client, Fortinet, and the feedback was positive.

Here is the problem we set out to solve. Today, when a client starts a Lumen implementation, we ask them to fill out a requirements document on their own. The trouble is, a lot of clients do not do it, or they send it back half finished or inconsistent. So the document that is supposed to speed onboarding up actually ends up slowing it down. It is a chore for the client, and the consultant spends days chasing missing details before they can even start.

Let me show you what we built instead. I will use Apple as an example client.

### Beat 1 — Sales pre-load  [TOOL, LIVE]  (about 1.5 minutes)

▸ Switch to the Sales page tab.

When our sales team talks to a client, they already gather a lot of this information. So the first thing the assistant does is let us load that in up front, so the client never has to repeat themselves.

▸ Paste the Apple brief into the free-form brief box.

Here is what sales gathered about Apple: their products, their markets, the competitors they want to watch, and their launch campaign.

▸ Click "Preview how the assistant reads this."

And before I send anything, I can check how the assistant understood it.

▸ The preview appears.

You can see it has picked up the brand, the competitors, the channels, and the campaign. No surprises, no bad data going in.

▸ Fill in Company as Apple, choose the package, language English. Click Generate link.

Now I generate the client's own private link, and that is what we send them.

### Beat 2 — The client opens the link  [TOOL, LIVE]  (about 1.5 minutes)

▸ Open the fresh Apple client link in a new tab.

This is what the client sees when they open their link.

▸ The assistant greets them and already references Apple and their context.

Notice it is not asking who they are or what they sell. It already knows, because we pre-loaded it. So the client starts from a warm, informed conversation instead of a blank form.

### Beat 3 — The conversation and how it adapts  [TOOL, LIVE]  (about 2 minutes)

It also adapts to how experienced the client is.

▸ Click the "Some experience" option.

A beginner gets more suggestions and hand-holding. A more experienced client gets more open questions, so we do not waste their time.

▸ Answer the goal question. Type something like: We want to protect our reputation around the next iPhone launch and keep an eye on Samsung and Google.

Now watch the panel on the right as I answer. The requirements document is building itself in real time: the objectives, the topics to track, the competitors.

▸ A topic suggestion appears and the side panel fills in.

Here it is already suggesting topics tied to exactly what they said: the iPhone launch, and the competitors they named.

### Beat 4 — It works in the client's language  [TOOL, LIVE]  (about 30 seconds)

One more thing. Our clients are not always working in English.

▸ Switch the conversation language to Arabic.

The whole conversation runs in the client's own language, live. Here it is in Arabic, right to left.

▸ Switch back to English.

Back to English for the rest.

### Beat 5 — The finished document  [TOOL, PREPARED]  (about 2 minutes)

Rather than have you watch me answer twenty questions, here is the same conversation finished. I ran it earlier this morning.

▸ Switch to the finished conversation tab.

After about a 15-minute conversation, we have a complete, consistent requirements document: their business objectives, what they want to track, the reports and alerts they need, and who should have access to the platform.

And to be clear, this does not replace our consultants. The client still meets with our team before implementation begins. The difference is that the consultant now starts with complete, consistent information, instead of chasing missing details.

### Beat 6 — What the team sees  [TOOL, LIVE]  (about 1 minute)

▸ Open the Dashboard tab.

And this is what the team sees across every onboarding: who has started, what languages they are using, and the time we are saving.

### Impact  [SLIDE]  (about 2.5 minutes)

So what is the impact.

Each onboarding saves a consultant about an hour of chasing and cleaning up requirements. We run around 750 of these a year. That is roughly 750 hours saved a year, which is close to half a full-time consultant, freed up for higher-value work with clients.

And that is just our side. It also saves the client time and frustration. They are guided step by step, they do not repeat themselves, and they are not filling in a form into the void. That means faster time to value and a better first experience, which helps us keep and grow those accounts.

### Query Builder and close  (about 1 minute)

Once we have this brief, the next step is turning it into the actual searches. A separate tool does that: it drafts the searches automatically, gives each one a confidence score, flags the assumptions to confirm with the client, and generates a go-live checklist. So the consultant spends their time validating with the client, instead of doing the initial work from scratch.

These are just two examples of how Professional Services is using AI to work smarter, so we have more time for our clients. And there is a lot more coming. Thank you.

## PASTE BLOCKS (for the Sales page during prep)

BRIEF (paste into the free-form brief box):

Company: Apple.
Key products: iPhone (the focus for this setup), plus Apple Watch and AirPods.
Markets: United States, United Kingdom, Germany.
Languages: English and German.
Main goals: protect brand reputation, watch key competitors, and track the next product launch.
Competitors to watch: Samsung, Google Pixel.
Social channels: Instagram (@apple), X (@apple), YouTube (Apple), TikTok (@apple).
Campaign: the upcoming iPhone launch, tracked around the launch event and its hashtag.
Concern: during past launches, price and battery complaints spread fast. The team wants early warning when negative mentions spike.
Reports they expect: a weekly brand health summary for the marketing lead, and a launch-day spike alert for negative sentiment.

CONSULTANT NOTES (paste into the notes box, this is never shown to the client):

Large, sophisticated brand, but the champion on this project is a product marketing manager focused on one launch. Keep it practical and launch-focused, not a boil-the-ocean setup.
The real priority is launch-day monitoring: past launches saw fast-moving price and battery complaints, so lead your suggestions on crisis and sentiment-spike alerting.
Competitive focus is Samsung's overlapping Galaxy launch window. Surface Samsung and Google Pixel as competitors to track if they only mention their own brand.
Keep the first setup tight and tied to the launch. They can expand later.
