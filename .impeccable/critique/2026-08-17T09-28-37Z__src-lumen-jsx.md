## Design Specificity Verdict

**Specific at its core, generic in its polish, and the investment is inverted.**

**Design review.** The shell is category-interchangeable: avatar-left/bubble-right transcript, pinned composer with paperclip and circular send, three-dot thinking bubble, outline quick-reply chips, header icon trio, 6-dot stepper, modal-with-accordions review. Swap the copy and this is a support bot, a tax intake, a mortgage pre-qual. Nothing in the composition says social listening.

Four things genuinely could not be lifted into another product:

- **The topic card** (`2269-2289`): name + Boolean keyword string + the assistant's rationale + keep/discard + drag-to-prioritise. That is a Boolean-query authoring object, meaningless anywhere else.
- **The purple-edged widget card** (`4395`) as a distinct object *inside* the transcript: a consistent "this turn wants a structured answer" signal that is neither a form nor a message. This is the one invention holding the "conversation, not a form" claim up.
- **Stepper labels in client language**: "What to track", "Where to look" rather than "Topics", "Channels" (`112`).
- **The readiness ring** keyed to the eight things a Lumen environment actually needs (`2682-2691`).

The problem is that the specific parts are the least designed. The topic card clips the keyword string it asks you to approve. The readiness ring counts presence, not specificity. The panel is a 14-row field list. Meanwhile the interchangeable chat shell is beautifully finished. Effort went where the product is generic and not where it is unique.

**On your two anti-references.** "A form wearing a chat costume" is not yet true of the prose — the replies read as a peer, the assistant pushes back, the first turn confirms rather than re-asks. It becomes true at three specific places: the moment the first widget appears and tone drops from dialogue to 17 chips; the side panel, which enumerates 14 named fields under the header "STILL TO CAPTURE" — a form's field list in a form's vocabulary, permanently on screen at ≥1280; and the review modal, which is literally a 30-field form. **The interface undoes the voice work.** "Survey or questionnaire" lands for the same reason: "Captured so far / Still to capture" is extraction vocabulary.

## Overall Impression

The conversational layer is good and the scaffolding around it is undoing it. The prose behaves like the knowledgeable peer you specified, the pre-brief is genuinely expressed in the interface rather than only in the prompt, and three pieces of engineering craft (the in-flight lockout, the advancing thinking copy, the retry banner) exist because someone watched a real client struggle.

Then the client cannot see the question. That is the single biggest opportunity, and it is one effect with a two-line cause.

Underneath it sits a subtler problem that matters more for your stated goal. **This interface is built to detect that an answer exists, not that an answer is good.** The readiness ring counts presence. The panel ticks fields. Skip is the only legible button on a fresh widget. There is no "I don't know" anywhere. For a product whose success metric is brief quality and whose positioning is "the assistant knows what a good answer looks like", that knowledge is never once on screen.

## What's Working

**1. The pre-brief is expressed in the interface, not just the prompt.** The `✦ Prepared for Acme Corp` chip, "Welcome, Jane!", "Start Acme Corp's setup", a bespoke tab title, and the one that actually matters: the USERS form arrives pre-filled from `cdata.company` (`4005-4009`). Four surfaces carry "we already know you" and none mentions the contract. That is your third principle built rather than promised.

**2. The in-flight widget lockout.** `aria-busy` + `opacity .55` + `pointerEvents:none` applied once at the wrapper for all five widget types (`4020-4021`), paired with thinking copy that advances and then *holds* rather than looping. Together they make a non-streaming black box legible. This only exists because someone watched a client tap Confirm twice.

**3. The chat retry banner** (`4419-4426`). Offline glyph, "That didn't go through. Tap Try again to resend.", one button, and the client's own message left intact above it with its Edit affordance. No blame, no error code, nothing lost. It is strictly better than the send-failure copy inside the review modal and should be that one's template.

## Priority Issues

### P0 — The client cannot see the question. Auto-scroll never fires for any reply taller than 200px.

`src/lumen.jsx:3114-3118`. The effect computes `nearBottom` from `scrollHeight - scrollTop - clientHeight < 200` **after** React has committed the new content, in the same pass where `loading` flips to `false`. Any turn adding more than 200px fails both branches, so nothing scrolls.

I reproduced this myself at 1280x720 after two ordinary turns: `scrollTop` **0**, `scrollHeight` 1680 against `clientHeight` 528, **1,152px unseen**, and the newest widget's Confirm button at **y=1723 in a 720px viewport**. Meanwhile the composer placeholder reads "Answer above — or just type it here", pointing at something not on screen.

**Why it costs brief quality:** the client answers only the part they can see and never reaches the structured widget, the quick replies, or the attach affordance. The paste-your-existing-queries path is behind an off-screen chip. A distracted client simply stops.

**Fix:** capture the distance-from-bottom in a ref *before* the message array changes and decide from that; or on assistant turns drop the heuristic and scroll the new message's **top** into view whenever the previous state was near-bottom. Anchor on the message, not `botRef`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### P0 — Reopening a submitted widget silently removes its answer from the delivered brief, with no Cancel.

Edit sets `submitted: false` while keeping `data` (`4002`), but both readers gate on `submitted`: the panel's `gwp` (`4054`) and the export's `gw` (`2625`). I confirmed this in source. The reopened widget offers only Confirm (restores) or Skip (destroys) — there is no Cancel.

**Why it costs brief quality:** this breaks two of your own principles at once — "never lose what the client typed" and "interruption is the normal case". Open Edit, get pulled into a meeting, come back or don't: the answer is gone from the export. The dashboard shows a session with markets missing and nobody knows the client already gave them.

**Fix:** read `data` regardless of `submitted` in both `gwp` and `gw`, treating reopened-with-data as still captured and optionally flagged "being edited". Add an explicit Cancel that restores `submitted: true`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`

### P1 — There is no "I don't know" path, and Skip is the only legible action on a fresh widget.

On every chip and ranked selector, before any selection, **Confirm** is `#e2e8f0` background with `#fff` text (`2149`), measured ~1.3:1 and functionally invisible, while **Skip** sits beside it fully legible (`2150`). At the max-3 limit the unpicked options go to 1.58:1 *and* `disabled`, so keyboard users cannot even reach them to learn they exist, and the explanation sits ~400px below, off-screen on mobile. On mobile Skip occupies the right edge of the footer: the optimal thumb target.

**Why it costs brief quality:** at first contact with every widget, the one button the client can read is the one that produces no data. Skip writes `Skipped MARKETS` and moves on, so the assistant never gets the chance to do the thing the product is built on — push back on a thin answer.

**Fix:** give disabled Confirm a real disabled treatment (border plus `#94a3b8` on `#f1f5f9`, legible) and a reason line, the way `UserForm` already does (`2235`). Demote Skip to a text link. Then change it from data-destroying to conversational: "I'm not sure" sends a message the assistant answers with an industry-specific example and re-offers the widget. Move the at-limit counter above the chips.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### P1 — The review modal certifies presence as readiness, hides the content, and styles the escape hatch as the primary action.

Three compounding problems at the last gate. `reqChecks` (`2682-2691`) is eight presence booleans, so a brief whose only topic is named "our brand" with keywords "acme" scores **100% · Ready to send · green**. In the happy path every section collapses (`2766`, `2794`, `2816`, `2827` default to open only when empty or unconfirmed), so "Everything you've shared, in one place" shows four collapsed rows and a percentage. And "Send it anyway" renders as full-purple primary (`2867`) while "Keep going" is a ghost button. This is also the noisiest surface in the detector sweep at 47 findings.

**Why it costs brief quality:** this is the mechanism by which a vague brief is officially blessed. It teaches the client that filled equals done, hides the evidence that would contradict it, then makes the shortcut the loudest button on screen.

**Fix:** score *specificity*, not presence — a topic with no capitalised entity or no operator, a market list containing both "Global" and a named country, an objective with an empty details field, a single user who is also the contact. Each becomes a named gap with copy the client can argue with: "Nova Audio is your only named competitor — most competitor watches need three." Default non-empty sections to open. Invert the two buttons.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable shape`

### P1 — The topic card clips the Boolean keyword string it asks the client to approve.

`2276-2277`. Name and keywords are single-line inputs at 326px. Measured: `"Acme Corp" OR AcmeCorp OR (Acme AND (earbuds OR` has `scrollWidth` 406 in a 326px box; the category card is 472 in 326. No wrap, no expand, no `title`, no character count. The assistant's prose asks "which of these is wrong?" about text the client cannot read. Neither input has an `aria-label`, so once filled the accessible name is empty.

**Why it costs brief quality:** this is *the* configuration artefact. A client who cannot read the query cannot correct it, so they confirm the AI's guess — exactly the "completed session, vague brief" failure your PRODUCT.md names.

**Fix:** make keywords a two-row auto-growing textarea with `white-space: pre-wrap`, a visible label ("Keywords Lumen will search for"), and tighter tracking so operators read as operators. Promote the rationale from 11px italic to 12px normal directly under the name; it is the judgement input. Demote the ☰ ▲ ▼ ✓ ✕ chrome to one icon group on the trailing edge.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable layout`

### P2 — Shrinking the window below 1280 strands content off the left edge with no way to scroll to it.

Verified independently at 768x800 after resizing down from 1280: the 320px panel stays `display:block`, the message column is pushed to `left:-96`, **10 elements sit off the left edge**, and there is no horizontal scroll to reach them (`scrollWidth === clientWidth === 768`). The stepper's step-1 label is at `left:-77`, cut in half. A fresh load at 768 is fine, so this is resize-only.

**Fix:** re-evaluate the panel's open state on resize, not only at mount, and force it closed below `SIDE_COL_MIN`.

**Suggested command:** `/impeccable adapt`

## Minor Observations

**WCAG 2.2 failures worth having on the record** (no formal target, so these are decisions, not blockers): once the conversation starts the page has **zero headings and zero landmarks** — no `<main>`, no `<h1>`, the 320px panel is a plain `div`, the stepper has no `progressbar` semantics. That is the cheapest high-value a11y fix in the file. Neither the retry banner nor the modal's send failure carries `role="alert"`, so both are silent to assistive tech. The three highest-value fields in the product (topic name, topic keywords, the queries textarea) are placeholder-labelled only. `TopicCards` aria-labels are hardcoded English, so five controls per card announce in English in a French, German, Spanish, Italian or Arabic session.

**Dark mode is unfinished rather than imperfect.** Every widget is hardcoded light and renders as a bright island inside the dark chat (`TopicCards` `2273`, `UserForm` `2209`, `RankedSelector` `2171`, `ChipSelector` `2144`, `QueriesWidget`). The entire `ExportModal` is hardcoded light (`2739`), so a dark-mode client gets a full-white flash at the send moment.

**RTL is mostly right, with two real defects.** Mirroring is genuinely good: panel docks left, avatar right, stepper reversed, composer send left, correct `dir`/`lang` on the root. But the widget hint applies `font-style: italic` to Arabic, which has no italic form, so browsers synthesise a mechanical slant that reads as broken rendering; and the bubble radius is flattened to a uniform 14 rather than mirrored, dropping the tail asymmetry that distinguishes assistant from client.

**Smaller things.** A floating scrollbar renders ~200px inside the content area because the scroll container is the translated message column. At 1280x720 the opening turn leaves ~350px of dead canvas above the composer. "✓ Submitted" shows no content while the answer appears in a differently-styled bubble below it — two visual languages for one act. `You chose: X` duplicates the bubble 60px below. On reload after sending, the receipt renders under a stepper reading "Step 1 of 6 · About you · 0%", contradicting "Brief sent". "Download a copy" disappears on the confirm-send step, so at the exact moment a send fails the client cannot keep a copy of their own answers. `TopicCards` says "Drag to prioritize" but never shows the resulting order. The `✓` keep button is a no-op on arrival, since all cards seed as kept. Confirm label drifts across widgets ("Confirm", "Confirm (3)", "Confirm priorities", "Confirm users", and "No queries" where every other widget says "Skip").

## Questions to Consider

1. **What if the readiness ring scored specificity instead of presence?** Would "Nova Audio is your only named competitor — most competitor watches need three" do more for brief quality than "88% · Almost there"? And would your consultants rather receive a 62% brief with honest gaps than a 100% brief with "our brand" as a topic?

2. **What if no widget ever asked a question, and only confirmed an answer?** Hold three exchanges in prose, let the assistant push back, and produce the widget only once it already knows roughly what the answer is — pre-selected, with "I've put US, UK and Germany, anything missing?". That single inversion kills the "form wearing a chat costume" reading outright, because every widget becomes a receipt rather than a question.

3. **Your positioning is that the assistant knows what a good answer looks like. Why is that knowledge never on screen?** Right now it is an 11px muted italic definition. What would one line of worked example from the client's own industry do, next to an "I'm not sure" button that asks the assistant instead of destroying the answer?

4. **If interruption is the normal case, why does "Welcome back" show a percentage instead of the thread?** What would it cost for the resume screen to read like a peer picking up: "Last time you gave me US, UK and Germany, named Nova Audio as the competitor that matters, and we'd got as far as where to look. Three things left."

5. **Who is the side panel for — the client, or the consultant looking over their shoulder?** "Captured so far / Still to capture" is extraction vocabulary and a 14-row tick-list is a form's field list. If it is for the client, why is it a checklist of your fields rather than a growing picture of their setup?
